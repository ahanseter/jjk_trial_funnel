The trial funnel leak

Where free-trial signups in SMS stall before becoming a tracked SAP order — built from the SAP BI EOI reconciliation export and live production subscription data.

Export window Jul 27 – Aug 18, 2026 Volume context Jan 1 – Aug 27, 2026 Generated Aug 27, 2026 For SMS product & ops
−15pts
SAP order creation ran at 70–74% of trials through Q1, then dropped to 55–59% in April and stayed there for four months. Okta account provisioning, meanwhile, has been flat all year — so this isn't general decay, it's a step-change with a date on it. Across 29,285 trials this year, that gap plus a chronic 14% Okta failure rate is where the funnel actually leaks.
01

Executive summary

Every SMS subscription — trial or paid — is meant to end up as a matched order in SAP. That's how JJK counts it, bills it, and routes it to a rep. The reconciliation export SMS runs against SAP's EOI extract exists to catch the ones that don't make it. In the latest three-week window, 771 trials landed on that list out of 2,081 created — roughly one in three.

Looking across all 29,285 external trials created this year, three drop-off points are unambiguous. 2.9% never get a user record at all. 14.2% get a user record but no Okta account — meaning they cannot sign in to the product they just signed up for. And 32.8% never get a SAP order attached. (A fourth measure, profile completion, is in dispute — see Section 03.)

The single largest finding is a step-change in April. SAP order creation ran at 70–74% through Q1, then fell to 55–59% in April and stayed there for four months. Okta provisioning, by contrast, has been flat all year. Something changed in April that is still costing roughly 15 points of order conversion on every trial. A candidate has since emerged — the April promo-code release triggered duplicate verification emails to ~10,000 recipients — but it has not been shown to account for the whole drop.

Two specific days broke outright. On July 9, Okta provisioning failed for ~57 people in three concentrated hourly windows — worst at 22:00, where only 2 of 22 trials got an account — while SAP ran normally. Marketing has since confirmed the Acoustic email template was changed that same day, which would explain it: no click, no Okta account. On July 15–16, the reverse pattern — Okta flawless, SAP order creation collapsed to under 4%, leaving ~200 trials with no order that appear never to have been backfilled.

Within the EOI export itself, the stuck trials don't recover on their own: 752 of 768 are still on a trial subscription type today and 647 have run past their end date without converting.

Separately — and this is the part most likely to be misread — volume and conversion are two different problems. Trials are down 45% since January, but SAP lead records are down 46% over the same period. Those moving together means the decline starts above the sign-up form: it is a demand problem, driven by paid-search volume falling ~57% and one agency campaign dropping 98%. The engineering faults below are real and cost real orders, but they are not what halved the volume, and fixing them will not bring it back. Section 08 separates the two.

Finally, this report documents what isn't measured. Stages 3 and 4 of the flow — the entire email-verification window — emit no telemetry at all, so anyone who abandons there leaves no trace in any system. Section 09 lists every gap.

Stage 6 is a gate, not a step — and that reframes the whole report. The SAP trial order and the welcome email are triggered only when the mandatory profile is completed for the first time. Abandon before stage 6 and there is no SAP order and no welcome email by design. So the correlation in Section 04 — that 99.6% of trials with an incomplete profile have no SAP match — is not evidence of a broken integration. It is the system behaving exactly as specified. The real question is not why SAP is missing records; it is why so few people finish the profile form.
Stages 3 and 4 emit no telemetry whatsoever. Between requesting a verification code and entering it, the application records nothing durable — the temporary record is deleted on use or after one hour, and no event is fired. The same is true if Terms of Use are declined. Anyone who abandons in that window leaves no trace in any system: not in the SMS database, not in Pendo, not in Elastic. This is the single largest measurement hole in the funnel, and it sits directly on top of the verification-email step we most want to understand after the July 9 template change.
Open question carried over from the process map: how long is a trial? The base configuration says 7 days; marketing copy says 30 days. The live value comes from a deployment setting that is not in source control, so it cannot be resolved by reading the code — someone has to check the running production configuration. Until it is settled, every expiry-based figure in this report inherits the ambiguity.

The April step-change is the story in this chart. Okta account creation is flat all year (81–85%, easing to 78.8% in August). SAP order creation, by contrast, ran at 70–74% through Q1 and then dropped to 55–59% from April onward — and stayed there for four months. A candidate cause emerged on Aug 27. The promo-code feature shipped in April — the same month as the step-change — and its Acoustic send wrote to a column that did not exist. This is a strong lead, not a proven cause — the exact start and end dates have not been pinned down, so no one has yet shown it accounts for the full 15-point drop. Confirming those dates is the fastest way to test it.

July 9 was an Okta problem, not a SAP problem. The failure is concentrated in three windows — 16:00, 17:00, and most severely 22:00, where only 2 of 22 trials got an Okta account. SAP order creation that same day ran at 71%, above the monthly average, so the order path was healthy throughout. Roughly 57 people who started a trial on July 9 ended up with a subscription and an order but no way to sign in. Those accounts are individually recoverable if someone provisions them.
And July 9 has a confirmed upstream event. Marketing confirmed on Aug 27 that the Acoustic email template was changed on July 9, altering where recipients could click. That gives a coherent chain: a changed template → fewer verification-email clicks → fewer Okta accounts created. It fits the timing exactly and explains why SAP was untouched while Okta was not. It is not yet proven — comparing Acoustic click rates for July 8 against July 9 would settle it quickly, and that data was not available for this report.
July 15–16 was the mirror image — and it is worse. Across those two days, Okta provisioning was flawless (100%: 108/108 and 98/98) while SAP order creation collapsed to 1.9% and 3.1% — 5 orders out of 206 trials. Every other day that month ran 50–75%. This has all the shape of a two-day SAP integration outage that, as far as this data shows, was never backfilled: those ~200 trials still have no order attached.

The decline is broad, with one outlier. Paid search fell roughly evenly across both networks — Bing 1,135→492 (−57%), Google 826→351 (−58%) — which looks like reduced spend or reduced bidding rather than any single campaign failing. The exception is TM SMS CM Trial placement Agency, which went from ~391/month in Q1 to 6 in August, collapsing in May. A similarly-named campaign (TM SMS trial placement Agency) rose from 30 to 164 over the same period, so this is probably a rename or re-contract — but the replacement is running at roughly 40% of the original volume, a net loss of ~200 leads/month. Someone in marketing will know in a minute whether that was deliberate.
The lead-to-trial ratio independently confirms the April step-change. Leads are created in SAP downstream of the trial order, so if SAP order creation breaks, lead records go missing too. That is exactly what the ratio shows: leads as a share of trials ran 84–89% in Jan–Mar, dropped to 68–75% from April, and recovered to 87% in August — tracking the measured SAP order rate (73%→56%→68%) almost exactly, from a completely separate dataset. Two independent sources agreeing on the same April break and the same August recovery is the strongest evidence in this report that the step-change is real.
So there are two distinct problems, and they compound. (1) Demand — roughly 45% fewer people are starting trials, driven by paid-search volume and the agency campaign change. That is a marketing question. (2) Conversion — since April, ~15 points fewer of those who do start end up as a counted order. That is an engineering question. Fixing the funnel will not bring the volume back; restoring the spend will not fix the leak. They need separate owners.

The front-end telemetry everyone assumes exists is not being collected. Elastic APM's browser agent is live in production — rum-js v5.17.4, reporting from www.jjkellerportal.com within the last hour. But over 90 days the SMS_Web_UI service has recorded 0 transactions and 0 spans, against 111,411 errors and 30.5M metric documents. No transactions means no page views, which means no front-end funnel can be built from it. Nonprod shows the same pattern (0 transactions, 12,688 errors), so this is not an environment-specific misconfiguration.
A likely cause, offered as a lead rather than a conclusion. apm.init() is called from the AppModule constructor rather than in main.ts before bootstrapModule(). Elastic's Angular integration expects initialisation before bootstrap; initialising later means the page-load transaction window has already closed and route-change instrumentation may never bind to the router. The error handler is registered separately and would keep working either way — which matches the errors-yes / transactions-no asymmetry exactly. This needs a developer to confirm; it was not verified beyond the data above.

Net effect: the profile-form abandonment question — do people reach the form and give up, or never arrive? — cannot be answered from any currently available source. Pendo Analytics is unlicensed, Elastic RUM records no page views, and Application Insights has not yet been checked.

11

Recommendations

Roughly in priority order — the first two are cheap and point straight at the biggest number here.

01
Now

Ask marketing what happened to paid search and the agency placement

This is the largest single number in the report and it is not an engineering issue. Paid search leads fell ~57% across Bing and Google, and TM SMS CM Trial placement Agency went from ~391/month to 6. If those were deliberate budget decisions, the volume decline is explained and the engineering work should be scoped accordingly. If they weren't, that is the most valuable thing anyone finds this week.

02
Now

Pin the exact start and end dates of the duplicate-email window

This is now the fastest test of the April step-change. If duplicate sends began with the April promo-code release and stopped when the missing column was added in late August, the dates should line up against the SAP order rate — and if they don't, the real April cause is still out there. Either answer is progress; right now TF-06 has a candidate and no verdict.

03
Now

Backfill the July 15–16 SAP outage and provision the July 9 Okta accounts

Two bounded, known lists: ~200 trials with no order from the SAP outage, and ~57 people from July 9 who have a subscription but no way to sign in. Both look recoverable by hand, and neither appears to have been cleaned up.

04
Now

Fix the retry logic, not just the missing column (TF-02)

Adding the column stopped today's duplicates, but the send still retries without checking which error it received. Re-enable promo codes and the same duplicate storm returns. This is currently logged as fixed; it is half-fixed, and the remaining half is the durable one.

05
Next

Attack profile completion as a conversion problem

Now that stage 6 is confirmed as the trigger for everything downstream, this stops being an integration question and becomes a form-design one: shorten it, pre-fill what sign-up and the promo code already supply, or stage the fields so the order fires earlier. Separately, the two competing definitions of “profile complete” (95% incomplete by location-address proxy, 25% by the EOI column) still need reconciling before either number is quoted.

06
Next

Alert on daily Okta and SAP completion rates

All four incident days in Section 04 were plainly visible in a daily rate and none appear to have been caught at the time. A simple threshold alert (say, either rate below 70% for a day) would have surfaced every one of them.

07
Next

Work the 104 recoverable records from the EOI export

These already have a SAP account and an order — only the link back to SMS is missing. Highest-value, lowest-effort segment in the export, and a bounded list.

08
Next

Validate address and region/country at profile-save time

Blank addresses and invalid region/country pairs account for most of the 33 blocked-but-matched records. Both are catchable in SMS before SAP ever sees the record.

09
Later

Investigate the chronic 14% Okta gap

Beyond the incident days, roughly one in seven trials never gets an Okta account at all — a rate that has held steady all year. Worth understanding whether these are abandoned signups or silent provisioning failures.

10
Later

Fix Elastic RUM transaction capture — it is the cheapest route to front-end visibility

The browser agent is already deployed, licensed and reporting; it just isn't recording page views (§08). If the apm.init() placement is the cause, this is a small change that unlocks the profile-form question, the signup funnel, and front-end performance data at once. Pendo can't fill this gap — SMS sends it no data at all — and Acoustic remains the only route to email-verification data.
